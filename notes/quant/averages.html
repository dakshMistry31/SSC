Averages

SSC CHSL Quantitative Aptitude — Topic Notes

📖 1. What is an Average?

An average represents the equal share of a total among a given number of observations.

Average = Sum of observations ÷ Number of observations
Sum = Average × Number of observations

🎯 2. What You Need to Learn

  • Simple average
  • Finding a missing number
  • Adding or removing observations
  • Replacement problems
  • Average of groups
  • Weighted average situations

🧮 3. Basic Average

Example 1: Find the average of 12, 18, 20, 10 and 15.
Sum = 75; number of values = 5. Average = 75/5 = 15.
Example 2: The average marks of 6 tests is 72. What is the total?
Total = 72×6 = 432.
Example 3: The average age of 4 friends is 19 years. Their total age is 19×4 = 76 years.

🔎 4. Finding a Missing Value

First find the required total from the average, then subtract the known values.

Example 1: Average of 5 numbers is 24. Four numbers are 18, 21, 25 and 27. Find the fifth.
Total = 24×5 = 120. Known sum = 91. Missing = 29.
Example 2: Average of 8 scores is 35. Seven scores total 230. Missing score = 35×8 − 230 = 50.
Example 3: Average monthly expense for 6 months is ₹4,500. Five months total ₹21,000. Sixth month = 27,000 − 21,000 = ₹6,000.

➕ 5. Adding an Observation

If the average of n observations is A and a new value x is added:

New average = (nA + x)/(n+1)
Example 1: Average of 5 numbers is 18. Add 28.
New average = (5×18+28)/6 = 118/6 = 19⅔.
Example 2: Average age of 9 students is 16. A new student aged 25 joins.
New average = (144+25)/10 = 16.9 years.

➖ 6. Removing an Observation

New average = (nA − removed value)/(n−1)
Example 1: Average of 6 numbers is 25. One number, 35, is removed.
New average = (150−35)/5 = 23.
Example 2: Average marks of 10 students is 64. One student scoring 82 leaves.
New average = (640−82)/9 = 62.

🔄 7. Replacement Method

When one value is replaced by another, the total changes only by the difference between the new and old values.

Change in average = (New value − Old value)/Number of observations
Example 1: In a group of 20, a value 35 is replaced by 55.
Average increases by (55−35)/20 = 1.
Example 2: In a class of 25 students, 48 is replaced by 38. Average decreases by 10/25 = 0.4.
Example 3: A player's score 42 is replaced by 62 in a 15-match record. Average increases by 20/15 = 4/3.

⚖️ 8. Combined Average

When groups have different sizes, do not simply average their averages. Find the total of each group first.

Combined average = (n₁A₁ + n₂A₂)/(n₁+n₂)
Example 1: 20 students average 60 and 30 students average 70.
Combined = (20×60+30×70)/50 = 66.
Example 2: 10 workers earn average ₹500/day and 15 workers earn ₹700/day.
Combined average = (5,000+10,500)/25 = ₹620.

⚠️ 9. Common Mistakes

  • Adding averages instead of totals.
  • Forgetting to change the number of observations after adding/removing a value.
  • Using simple average for groups of unequal size.
  • Ignoring the difference in replacement questions.

📌 10. CHSL Focus

Averages are explicitly included in the SSC CHSL Quantitative Aptitude syllabus. The key skill is converting quickly between average and total and handling additions, removals, replacements and grouped data.

🔄 11. Quick Revision

  • Average = Total ÷ Number.
  • Total = Average × Number.
  • Replacement questions are often fastest using the change-in-average formula.
  • For combined averages, weight each average by its group size.
