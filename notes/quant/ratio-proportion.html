Ratio & Proportion

SSC CHSL Quantitative Aptitude — Topic Notes

📖 1. What is Ratio?

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind in the same units. It tells us how many parts of one quantity correspond to another.

Ratio of a to b = a:b = a/b, where b ≠ 0

Before forming a ratio, convert quantities to the same unit.

🧮 3. Simplifying a Ratio

Divide both terms by their greatest common factor.

Example: Simplify 36:48.
HCF = 12 → 36÷12 : 48÷12 = 3:4.
Unit warning: 2 m : 50 cm is not 2:50. Convert 2 m = 200 cm, so the ratio is 200:50 = 4:1.

⚖️ 4. Proportion

Four quantities are in proportion when the ratio of the first pair equals the ratio of the second pair.

a:b = c:d ⇔ a/b = c/d ⇔ ad = bc
Example: 3:5 = x:20.
3/5 = x/20 → 60 = 5x → x = 12.

🧩 5. Dividing a Quantity in a Ratio

If a total amount is divided in the ratio a:b, total parts = a+b.

First share = Total × a/(a+b)
Second share = Total × b/(a+b)
Example: Divide ₹700 in the ratio 3:4.
Total parts = 7. First share = 700×3/7 = ₹300. Second = 700×4/7 = ₹400.

🔗 6. Combining Ratios

If A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5, make the B terms equal before combining.

Method: LCM of 3 and 4 is 12.
A:B = 8:12 and B:C = 12:15.
Therefore, A:B:C = 8:12:15.

📈 7. Direct Proportion

When one quantity increases in the same proportion as another, they are directly proportional.

x ∝ y → x/y = constant
Example: If 5 notebooks cost ₹100, then 10 notebooks cost ₹200, assuming the price per notebook remains constant.

📉 8. Inverse Proportion

When one quantity increases while another decreases so that their product remains constant, they are inversely proportional.

x ∝ 1/y → xy = constant
Example: If 6 workers complete a job in 10 days, then, under the same work conditions, 12 workers would take 5 days. Workers × days remains constant.

🛠️ 9. Useful Method

For word problems, first identify exactly what is being compared. Write the quantities with units, convert units if necessary, form the ratio, simplify, and then apply the appropriate proportion.

✏️ 10. Solved Examples

Example 1: Boys:girls = 3:2 and total students = 40.
Total parts = 5. Boys = 40×3/5 = 24; girls = 16.
Example 2: If 8 pens cost ₹120, what is the cost of 15 pens at the same rate?
Cost per pen = 120/8 = ₹15. Cost = 15×15 = ₹225.

⚠️ 11. Common Mistakes

  • Comparing quantities in different units.
  • Forgetting to reduce a ratio to its simplest form when required.
  • Using direct proportion when the relationship is inverse.
  • Adding ratio terms instead of using the total number of parts.
  • Combining ratios without making the common term equal.

🔄 13. Quick Revision

  • Convert units before forming a ratio.
  • a:b = c:d means ad = bc.
  • To divide a total in a:b, use a/(a+b) and b/(a+b).
  • Direct proportion: ratio remains constant.
  • Inverse proportion: product remains constant.
