Percentage

SSC CHSL Quantitative Aptitude — Topic Notes

📖 1. What is Percentage?

Percentage means “per hundred”. It expresses a quantity as a fraction out of 100. Percentage questions are among the most useful arithmetic questions because the same concept appears in profit and loss, discount, interest, data interpretation and comparisons.

Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100

🧮 4. Finding a Percentage of a Number

x% of N = (x/100) × N
Example: Find 15% of 240.
15/100 × 240 = 36. Answer: 36.

📈 5. Percentage Increase / Decrease

Percentage Increase = (Increase / Original Value) × 100
Percentage Decrease = (Decrease / Original Value) × 100
Example: A number increases from 200 to 250.
Increase = 50. Percentage increase = 50/200 × 100 = 25%.

🛠️ 6. Successive Percentage Change

If a value changes successively by a% and b%, the net percentage change is:

Net change = a + b + (ab/100)

Use signs: increase is positive and decrease is negative.

Example: A value increases by 20% and then decreases by 10%.
Net change = 20 − 10 + (20 × −10)/100 = 10 − 2 = 8% increase.

🔄 7. Reverse Percentage

If the final value after an increase or decrease is known, work backward from the percentage multiplier.

After x% increase → Final = Original × (100+x)/100
After x% decrease → Final = Original × (100−x)/100
Example: After a 20% increase, a number becomes 360.
Original × 120/100 = 360 → Original = 360 × 100/120 = 300.

⚖️ 8. Percentage Comparison

When comparing A with B, always identify the base value.

A is what % of B = (A/B) × 100
A is x% more than B = ((A−B)/B) × 100
Example: 60 is what percentage of 80?
60/80 × 100 = 75%.

⚡ 9. Useful Mental Shortcuts

  • 10% = divide by 10.
  • 5% = half of 10%.
  • 1% = divide by 100.
  • 20% = one-fifth.
  • 25% = one-fourth.
  • 50% = one-half.
  • 75% = three-fourths.

⚠️ 10. Common Mistakes

  • Using the new value instead of the original value as the base for percentage change.
  • Adding successive increases/decreases without considering the second change's base.
  • Confusing “A is 20% more than B” with “B is 20% less than A”.
  • Forgetting that percentage is always relative to a base.
