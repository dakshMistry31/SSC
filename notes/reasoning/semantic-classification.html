Semantic Classification

SSC CHSL General Intelligence & Reasoning — Topic Notes

📖 1. What is Semantic Classification?

Classification questions give a group of words or concepts. Most belong to one common category or share a common relationship, while one does not. Your task is to identify the odd one out.

Find the common property shared by the majority, then identify the item that does not follow it.

🎯 2. What You Need to Learn

  • Category-based classification
  • Profession/object/place classification
  • Living/non-living classification
  • Functional relationships
  • Meaning-based groups
  • Odd-one-out based on a precise property

🛠️ 3. Method

  1. Look at all options together.
  2. Find a property common to at least three.
  3. Check whether the fourth also satisfies it.
  4. If multiple properties seem possible, choose the most direct and standard classification.
Do not choose based only on appearance of the words. Classification is about meaning or category.

🌍 4. Category-Based Classification

Example 1: Apple, Mango, Banana, Carrot
Apple, mango and banana are fruits; carrot is a vegetable. Answer: Carrot.
Example 2: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Nepal
Delhi, Mumbai and Kolkata are Indian cities; Nepal is a country. Answer: Nepal.
Example 3: Tiger, Lion, Leopard, Cow
The first three are wild big cats; cow is a domestic herbivore. Answer: Cow.

🧰 5. Function-Based Classification

Example 1: Pen, Pencil, Marker, Chair
Pen, pencil and marker are writing instruments; chair is furniture.
Example 2: Knife, Scissors, Blade, Spoon
Knife, scissors and blade are primarily cutting tools; spoon is a serving/eating utensil.
Example 3: Keyboard, Mouse, Scanner, Notebook
The first three are computer input devices; notebook is not an electronic input device.

👥 6. Profession / Role Classification

Example 1: Doctor, Engineer, Teacher, Hospital
Doctor, engineer and teacher are professions; hospital is a place/institution.
Example 2: Judge, Lawyer, Clerk, Court
Judge, lawyer and clerk are roles/persons; court is a place/institution.
Example 3: Pilot, Driver, Sailor, Vehicle
Pilot, driver and sailor are people/occupations; vehicle is an object.

🧠 7. Concept-Based Classification

Example 1: Honesty, Truthfulness, Integrity, Anger
The first three are positive moral qualities; anger is an emotion.
Example 2: Summer, Winter, Monsoon, Monday
The first three relate to seasons/weather periods; Monday is a day of the week.
Example 3: Square, Triangle, Circle, Red
The first three are geometrical shapes; red is a colour.

⚠️ 8. Common Mistakes

  • Choosing an option simply because it is less familiar.
  • Finding a relationship shared by only two options.
  • Ignoring the exact category represented by the majority.
  • Overthinking when a standard category is obvious.

🔄 10. Quick Revision

  • Find the common category first.
  • Three options normally share the target property and one does not.
  • Use meaning, function, category or relationship—not spelling alone.
  • If two interpretations seem possible, prefer the most standard and specific one.
