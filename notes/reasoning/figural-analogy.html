Figural Analogy

SSC CHSL General Intelligence & Reasoning — Topic Notes

📖 1. What is Figural Analogy?

Figural analogy questions use diagrams or figures instead of words or numbers. You identify the transformation from the first figure to the second and apply the same transformation to the third figure.

Figure A → Figure B :: Figure C → ?

Typical transformations include rotation, reflection, movement, addition/removal of elements, change in shading, change in position and combinations of these.

🎯 2. What You Need to Observe

  • Direction of rotation
  • Mirror/reflection changes
  • Movement of dots, arrows or lines
  • Number of elements
  • Shading or filled/unfilled parts
  • Size and position
  • Combination or separation of shapes

🔄 3. Rotation

A figure may rotate by a fixed angle, commonly 90°, 180° or 270°.

Example 1: If an arrow pointing up becomes an arrow pointing right, the transformation is a 90° clockwise rotation. Apply the same rotation to the third arrow.
Example 2: If a triangle's top vertex moves to the bottom after the first transformation, check whether the complete figure has undergone a 180° rotation, then apply that rotation.
Example 3: If a four-sided figure changes orientation twice by the same quarter-turn, the rule is likely repeated 90° rotation, not a change in shape.

🪞 4. Mirror Reflection

In a vertical mirror, left and right positions reverse while top and bottom remain in their corresponding vertical positions.

Example 1: An arrow pointing right is mirrored in a vertical mirror → it points left.
Example 2: A dot on the left side of a square is reflected vertically → it appears on the right side.
Example 3: A shape with an asymmetric mark at the upper-left is vertically mirrored → the mark moves to the upper-right.

➕ 5. Addition / Removal of Elements

Sometimes the first transformation adds or removes one component.

Example 1: A circle containing one dot becomes a circle containing two dots. If the same rule applies, a third figure with two dots should become one with three dots.
Example 2: A square with three lines loses one line to become a square with two lines. Apply the same one-line removal to the next figure.
Example 3: A triangle gains one small circle after transformation. The corresponding third figure should also gain one small circle.

🎨 6. Shading / Position Changes

Example 1: If an unshaded sector becomes shaded while all positions remain unchanged, the transformation is a shading change.
Example 2: If a black dot moves from top to bottom while the shape remains identical, the rule is a position shift.
Example 3: If alternate parts change from shaded to unshaded, check for an alternating shading rule before considering rotation.

🛠️ 7. Fast Solving Checklist

  1. Compare the number of elements.
  2. Compare their positions.
  3. Check rotation/reflection.
  4. Check shading.
  5. Check addition/removal.
  6. Apply the simplest complete transformation.

⚠️ 8. Common Mistakes

  • Rotating only one element instead of the entire figure.
  • Confusing a mirror image with a rotation.
  • Ignoring a small dot, line or shaded region.
  • Choosing an option that matches shape but not orientation.
